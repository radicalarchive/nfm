● Not dumb, but I'd keep the zips. Concretely:

  Against pre-unzipping:
  - It's 279 files (84 models + 139 images + 56 sounds). Unzipped means that
  many HTTP requests — loadbase iterates all 84 models on every boot regardless,
  so that's not a lazy-loading win, it's 84 round trips instead of 1.
  - data/ is shared with the desktop game. Unzipping means either scattering
  loose files where the desktop build doesn't expect them, or duplicating ~1.7MB
  into js/ and now having two copies to keep in sync.
  - All three zips are Defl:N (deflate), so DecompressionStream('deflate-raw')
  handles them natively — no library, no wasm. The reader is a central-directory
  parse plus a stream pipe, roughly 80 lines, written once.

  For pre-unzipping: it's genuinely simpler, and it's the obvious move if the
  zip reader fights me.

  The deciding factor is that this isn't a fork in the road. If the reader
  misbehaves I can unzip -d and swap the VFS backend in about five minutes — the
  call sites all go through one readBytes(name) function either way. So I'd
  rather spend the 80 lines and keep data/ untouched and byte-identical to what
  the desktop game and the CheerpJ build already read.

  One real exception: sounds.zip is the case where you're right. It's the
  biggest, and if the MOD player ends up wanting random access to individual
  samples, decompressing 339KB to reach one sound is wasteful. But audio is
  deferred out of the spike, so I'd defer that decision too.
