  Your four points

  - Stats moved right, vertically stacked — six labelled rows (Top Speed →
  Damage) in a fixed right-hand column, same shape as the deployed launcher's
  car card.
  - Panes are free-height now. The car pane is as tall as its stat column
  (~7rem), stage is short, option rows are half-height. Nothing forces a common
  height.
  - Caret gone everywhere; the box treatment alone marks focus.
  - Enter is inert on value rows. It only acts on the main menu and on Start
  Race. Left/right is the entire interaction on a value row.

  Options, at scale. I made each option its own focusable row inside a labelled
  group, rather than one pane cycling presets — Opponents / Laps / Difficulty /
  Music, each with its own ◂ ▸. Up/down walks straight through them as part of
  the same vertical list, so nothing is nested or modal, and a tenth option
  costs a row instead of a redesign. The group caps at ~11rem and scrolls
  (scrollIntoView on move keeps focus visible), with an "All settings ▸"
  affordance in the group header for the long tail that belongs on the Settings
  page. The tradeoff is vertical space: four options is comfortable, eight
  starts scrolling — at which point the split between "race options" here and
  everything else in Settings is the thing to tune, not the layout.

  New slots 2 and 4:

  - 2 Glitch — datamosh. Backdrop is a dark scanband field with slices tearing
  sideways on a stuttered timeline. Selection splits the box's frame into
  cyan/magenta copies that jitter on stepped keyframes, the whole box shifts a
  couple of pixels, and the type carries the same chromatic split.
  - 4 Quiet — the sober one, deliberately: light, flat, generous spacing, one
  blue accent, near-invisible backdrop. Selection is an accent rail that wipes
  down the left edge, a 1px lift, and a soft ring that breathes. It's the
  counterweight to the other four — and it's the only style here that a
  thirty-row settings screen survives, which is worth knowing before you pick.
