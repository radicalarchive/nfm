  Cause: sortcars is only well defined up to stage 27. Past that, both car
  pickers run off the 16-car roster — the forced opponent is 7 + (n-10+1)/2,
  which is 16 at stage 28, and the rejection sampler's range 24 + 8*((n-10)/17)
  reaches 34, which the -= 16 fixup only brings back to 18. models[sc[j]] is
  then null and loadstage dies on .grat.

  The Java is identical here and never hits it: stage select clamps at 27
  (xtGraphics.java:1899, plus the right-arrow guard at :2597), and 28–32 are the
  multiplayer stages — Lobby.java:3839 labels them "Multiplayer - Stage 1..5".
  In multiplayer the grid comes over the network and sortcars isn't called at
  all.

  So the clamp is inside sortcars, affecting only the solo opponent pick.
  Multiplayer, when you add it, won't route through this. Stages 1–27 are
  byte-identical to before.

● You were right, and it was worse than the slider: nothing I'd done to the
  music volume was audible.

  audio.setMasterVolume drives the gain param with setValueAtTime +
  linearRampToValueAtTime. An AudioParam under automation ignores later
  gain.value = v assignments — the write succeeds, reads back correct, and is
  inaudible. That was the branch applyVolume always took. On top of that, the
  tracker calls setMasterVolume(1) itself on play and again for MOD effect 0x10,
  so anything set before playback got overwritten anyway.

  So ?musicvol= never did anything at any value, and the MUSIC_TRIM constant was
  equally inert — halving it twice changed nothing you could hear. What you've
  been listening to all along is the untrimmed, full-volume bus.

● Both committed, and deployed.

  The music fix is 73c22ca.

  The fps cap (94de8cc) had a compounding-drift bug. The deadline was
  nextFrameAt = now + 1000/MAX_FPS — set from now, so each frame's overshoot got
  folded into the next deadline. On a 60Hz display a 33.3ms deadline lands just
  past a vsync, so the frame waits for the one after it, and the error
  accumulates. Now the deadline advances by a whole interval from the previous
  deadline, with 2ms of slop for rAF timestamp jitter (a deadline missed by a
  fraction of a millisecond otherwise costs a full vsync) and a resync when more
  than an interval behind.

  Simulated against a jittered 60Hz timestamp stream:

  │ 20     │ 16.8   │ 20.0  │

  will average correctly but space frames unevenly; that's inherent to skipping
  whole frames.

  The bug

  The one that mattered was worth the night. Mad.drive repairs a car instantly
  when contO.dist === 0, and animates it over eight ticks otherwise — and dist
  is draw's output, zero for a car the camera culled. So the game asked "is 
  anyone looking at this car?" and then changed the simulation: fcnt reaching 7
  or 8 is what resets hitmag/cntdest/dest, i.e. what puts a wasted car back in
  the race. Two players, two cameras, so the same car re-entered the race on
  different ticks on each machine and everything downstream drifted.

  Three more of the same shape fell out alongside it: fcnt was only advanced
  inside the on-screen test, so cars repaired only while visible; stat()
  increments mad.missedcp which drive() reads back, making a HUD countdown into
  simulation state; and an interpolated redraw was rebuilding wasted cars,
  consuming the newcar flag on whichever client drew more frames.

  That's ten desync fixes total, every one of them presentation reaching into
  physics. The general rule is now in WORK.md: ContO.dist is draw's one genuine 
  output and nothing in the simulation may branch on it.
