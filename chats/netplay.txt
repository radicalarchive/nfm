>>> you think its possible to adapt the old netcode or rewrite it to use webrtc? would be very cool to have multiplayer


Yes, and the port's architecture is unusually well-suited to it — but I'd rewrite rather than adapt.

**Why the old netcode can't be reused directly.** `Lobby.java` and `Login.java` are TCP socket clients talking to servers that have been dead for years, and a browser can't open raw TCP at all. So the transport has to be replaced no matter what. What *is* worth mining from those files is the wire semantics — what the game considered authoritative, how it handled joins mid-race — not the code.

**The good news is the sim shape.** This game is close to ideal for deterministic lockstep:

- Fixed 18.9 Hz timestep already, with the accumulator in `main.js`.
- All 8 players are simulated locally every tick — `simulate()` loops `colide`/`drive`/`preform` over `nplayers`. A remote player is just a `Control` whose fields come off the wire instead of from `preform()` (AI) or the keyboard.
- `Control` is ~7 bits of input per player per tick (up/down/left/right/handb/enter/lookback). At 18.9 Hz that's under 20 bytes/sec per player. Bandwidth is a non-issue.
- Physics is **0.6–2.5 ms/tick**. That makes rollback genuinely affordable — re-simulating 5 ticks costs ~10 ms, well inside a 53 ms budget. Most projects can't do rollback because their sim is too expensive; here it's the cheap half.
- `Mad.colide()` already reads `xt.multion` and swaps in different constants (28000/1.27 vs 7000/1.0), so the multiplayer physics variant is already ported.

**The blockers are all determinism, and they're known.**

1. `Medium.random()` bottoms out in unseeded `Math.random()` — already on `TASKS.md` as a verification blocker, but it's a hard prerequisite for lockstep. Everything downstream of it (`contO.zy`/`xy`) diverges immediately.
2. `Medium` builds `tsin`/`tcos` at init from `Math.sin`/`Math.cos` (360 entries each). Those are **not** guaranteed bit-identical across JS engines — V8 and SpiderMonkey can differ in the last ulp, and you'd have players on both. Bake the 720 float32 values as literal constants and that risk disappears.
3. Everything else is already in good shape, and that's not luck: `TRANSPILE_SPEC`'s discipline of wrapping every int op with `i32`/`Math.imul` and every float op with `fr()` is exactly what bit-determinism requires. IEEE guarantees the rest. The `Math.imul`-on-a-float bug we just fixed is the kind of thing that would have shown up as a desync instead of a visual glitch.

**Transport.** WebRTC DataChannel in unreliable/unordered mode, with each packet carrying the last ~8 ticks of input so a drop self-heals without retransmission — standard for lockstep. You still need a signalling server for SDP exchange (a tiny WebSocket service), though for a first prototype you can literally copy-paste offer/answer strings between two browsers and skip the server entirely.

**The alternative** is host-authoritative state sync — host simulates, broadcasts positions. It sidesteps determinism completely, but costs much more bandwidth and needs good interpolation to look right, and interpolation is currently the port's weakest area (the car-jitter bug). Given how cheap the sim is, lockstep is the easier road here.

**What you'd still be missing:** the lobby, chat and player-list UI live in the ~9600 unported lines of `xtGraphics`, and the 11 skipped branches in `stat()` are exactly the multiplayer HUD. So a playable 2-player race is much closer than a finished multiplayer *mode* — you'd want a URL-param join for the prototype and leave the menus for later.
