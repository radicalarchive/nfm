● Lockstep gives you perfect determinism and free AI sync at the cost of gating
  every peer on the laggiest one, an N(N-1)/2 mesh, and no drop-in joins — while
  host-authoritative state sync (what the original UDPMistro does) tolerates
  latency and drop-in but needs dead reckoning and correction-hiding, which your
  determinism work has already made accurate enough to be invisible.

  Two things worth noting before you pick a direction:

  - The lockstep prototype already syncs 8000 ticks under loss in netloop.mjs,
  so the sim work isn't wasted either way — switching costs netsync.js + its 12
  tests (~20-25% of netplay), and netpeer.js, the launcher UI and every
  determinism fix survive.
  - TASKS.md:353 says reconsider lockstep before a 3rd player, and the
  browser-level sync check (netCheck()) is knowingly broken — it reports 0
  desyncs while performing 0 comparisons. If you rework the architecture, that
  gets replaced anyway; if you don't, it needs fixing first or you're flying
  blind.

  Which do you want to grind on — the netcode rework, or wiring the new slab GUI
  (which has the MP browser/lobby screens mocked already, and would give
  netplay a real front end)?

  On topology, if you want a recommendation: star through the host, not a mesh.
  State sync makes it natural — guests send one record (their own car)
  regardless of N, only the host holds N connections, and guest upload stays
  flat. Lockstep would have needed N(N−1)/2; this needs N−1.

  One subtlety worth deciding up front: the host should relay guests' packets

  instant, and it works with netstate.js completely unchanged — accepts() is

  reassigning bots on disconnect, is a decision rather than a port.

❯ pause for a moment.  what is unidiomatic about the current design?            

● Pausing — and one thing from the run that just finished, since it bears on the
  answer: ?draw=0 worked (verts 19,400 → 8–22, tick rates 7.4–19.4 → 16–20/s),
  but guest 2's browser died at ~30s (undefined readout, only 6 corrections).
  The PASS is real but that client's numbers aren't.

  Four things are unidiomatic, roughly in order of how much they'd bother
  someone who does this professionally:

  1. I extrapolate where the textbook interpolates. The standard state-sync
  design (Valve's latency-compensation writeup, Gaffer's snapshot interpolation)
  renders remote entities ~100ms in the past, interpolating between two
  snapshots that have both already arrived. I do the opposite: apply the newest
  snapshot immediately and run physics forward from it. That's why I have a
  600-unit snap to smooth away — the idiomatic design doesn't have a correction
  to hide, because it never guesses ahead. My "render-side smoothing" TODO is
  really a workaround for choosing extrapolation.

  2. Full snapshots every tick, no delta compression. 83 bytes per car
  unconditionally, whether or not anything changed. The idiom is delta-encoding
  against the last snapshot the receiver acked (Quake 3 onward). A parked car
  costs the same as a mid-air backflip, and nlaps/clear/pcleared change maybe
  once a lap and ship 18.9 times a second.

  3. No clock sync or RTT estimation. I assume "tick N means the same moment on
  both machines" with nothing measuring or correcting it. The run above is
  direct evidence against that assumption: clients sat at 16.2 vs 20.0 tick/s.
  Per-slot staleness ordering still works, but nobody knows how stale a packet
  is, so netstate.js can't weight a correction by its age.

  4. No protocol version, ack, or loss signal. One MSG_STATE byte and no version
  field, so a stale client silently misreads the field layout rather than
  refusing to join.

  Worth separating out: running full physics for dead reckoning is unusual —
  most games extrapolate with simple velocity — but here it's earned rather than
  sloppy. The determinism work makes it near-exact, and the measurement shows
  it: host-owned bots reconcile at 0.0 units residual. That one I'd defend.

  The one I'd actually change first is #1, because #2 and #4 are optimisations
  and hygiene, while #1 is the thing generating the visible artifact.
